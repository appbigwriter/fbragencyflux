## 5. Content architecture

### Pillars

1. **Read the label** — ingredient, serving, variant, source strength, and what a listing does or does not establish
2. **Build a sustainable routine** — movement, sleep, rest, comfort, and practical questions without individualized prescription
3. **Make the evidence legible** — explain study type, population, limitation, and uncertainty in plain English
4. **Life after forty, without the panic** — editorial framing around changing priorities, budget, access, and personal context; no age-deficit narrative
5. **What we do not know** — explicit unknowns, research gaps, and questions to take to a qualified professional

Recommended mix: 40% evidence/label literacy, 25% routine and habit context, 20% editorial perspective, 15% community questions. This is a planning ratio, not a forecast or performance claim.

### Formats

- Instagram/Pinterest: source cards, carousels, checklists, quote-free explainers, article trailers, alt-text-first static graphics
- Instagram/TikTok/YouTube Shorts: 20–60 second “one distinction” explainers, captioned and source-led
- YouTube: 4–8 minute explainers, occasional source walk-throughs, no faux-vlog testimony
- Facebook: adapted article excerpts, discussion prompts, public source links, Page updates
- Stories: polls about questions, source reminders, “what remains unknown,” link stickers where available
- Live/video: defer until moderation, disclosure, escalation, recording, and comment controls are approved

### Editorial copy rules

- Prefer `may`, `can be associated with`, `the study examined`, `the listing states`, and `ask a qualified professional`
- Separate E1 general ingredient/context evidence, E2 product/listing facts, and E3 marketplace perception
- A manufacturer or listing can establish what it says, not independent efficacy or safety
- Never use cure, treatment, miracle, guaranteed results, fabricated before/after, fabricated first-person result, false scarcity, unsupported superlatives, or invented credentials
- No “I tried this for a month” unless a real, documented human author actually did so and the claim is approved; Heidi cannot make this claim

## 8. Organic follower acquisition

1. Make the profile immediately legible: publication name, FBR News relationship, fictional digital-persona disclosure, category scope, and one canonical destination
2. Create series people can recognize: `Label, listing, or evidence?`, `What remains unknown`, and `One better question`
3. Optimize for saves, shares, qualified comments, returning viewers, and site sessions—not empty follower volume
4. Reply to useful public questions with source-backed, general education; turn recurring questions into reviewed posts
5. Participate in relevant communities only with explicit admin permission and genuine contribution; no spam, scraping, mass follow/unfollow, or unsolicited DMs
6. Cross-link approved content from FBR News properties only after owner approval and with consistent disclosures
7. Use accessible captions, readable type, alt text, transcripts, and plain English for global readers
8. Test one variable at a time where feasible: hook, format, topic, or CTA. Record the test window and denominator before interpreting it
9. Collaborate with qualified, real creators only after identity, audience relevance, claims, compensation, rights, and disclosure are reviewed
10. Never buy followers, engagement, reviews, or “guaranteed reach”
11. Treat transparency itself as the growth asset: pin the disclosure, repeat it naturally when users ask, and let the digital-character format be a reason to follow the editorial series—not a reason to simulate a private life

## 10. Community management and moderation

## 13. Persona, health/wellness, and commercial risk controls

### Persona risks

- Users may mistake an illustrated editorial voice for a real reviewer or expert
- First-person grammar, photorealistic portraits, “I used this,” and lifestyle scenes can imply fabricated lived experience
- Reposts can strip context and disclosure
- A creator or commenter may falsely attribute an endorsement to Heidi
- Platform AI labels and policies can change; document the actual label used, but do not rely on it as the sole disclosure
- Synthetic media may be required to carry a platform-specific label, and the requirement can vary by platform, format, jurisdiction, and whether the asset appears realistic

Mitigation: put `Heidi Braun is a fictional digital editorial persona created by FBR News.` in bio/About and the pinned transparency post; repeat a concise version in captions or video frames where misunderstanding is likely; use any applicable platform synthetic-content label; avoid personal-result language; use publication-first visual identity. If comments treat Heidi as a real person, correct gently with the disclosure and do not role-play a private biography.

### Digital-character comment protocol

- `Heidi is real`, `where does she live?`, or similar: `Heidi Braun is a fictional digital editorial persona created by FBR News. The character frames the publication; scenes are illustrative, not a private life or personal testimony.`
- Requests for personal history, clinical credentials, private contact, or live personal experience: do not invent an answer; state the editorial boundary and route substantive questions to the publication
- Suspicious impersonation or accounts claiming to be the “real Heidi”: preserve a sanitized URL, report through the platform, and escalate to Sergio
- Do not mass-delete good-faith confusion; correct, pin, and improve the disclosure unless the comment is spam, abuse, doxxing, or impersonation

### Health/wellness rules

Before any publication, Gabe must classify each statement:

- `E1`: ingredient/general context supported by an appropriate source
- `E2`: product/listing identity or field observed, attributed and dated
- `E3`: marketplace perception/review, clearly labeled as perception, never efficacy or safety proof

Block or rewrite statements that:

- diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, or imply replacement for professional care
- promise weight loss, pain relief, muscle gain, cognition, recovery, safety, or a time-bound result
- give an individualized dose, routine, contraindication assessment, or “safe for everyone” instruction
- convert a label, certification, review, or manufacturer claim into independent proof
- use a fabricated testimonial, before/after, or Heidi’s first-person experience

Use a narrower educational statement, name the limitation, link the source, and recommend professional advice for personal circumstances. The mandatory disclaimer is a floor, not proof that a claim is compliant.

### Commercial and influencer controls

- Affiliate, gifted, paid, or brand relationship must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously in the content itself; use the platform’s native tool where offered but do not rely on it alone
- Influencer selection requires real identity, audience relevance, content history, contract, rights, claim boundaries, compensation, disclosure, and monitoring plan
- No fabricated reviews, incentivized undisclosed testimonials, or creator claims beyond evidence
- Contests/giveaways require written rules, eligibility, platform disclaimer, prize funding, privacy handling, moderation, and Sergio Gate before announcement
