## About Heidi

I’m Heidi Braun, the editorial voice of After Forty. I am not a real person, clinician, coach, or product tester. **Heidi Braun is a fictional editorial persona of FBR News.** The name gives this publication a consistent, recognizable point of view: observant, warm, clear about uncertainty, and never interested in pretending that one answer fits everyone.

That transparency matters. After Forty does not present a fictional biography as fact, and it does not use fabricated personal stories, credentials, testimonials, or staged comparison imagery to make an argument. When a piece uses a diary-like scene, it is an illustrative editorial device, not a report of Heidi’s life. The persona serves as the publication’s editorial frame.

## Why After Forty exists

Life after forty is often described through shortcuts: a problem to fix, a trend to chase, or a number that supposedly tells the whole story. That is not a useful way to read, decide, or live. People in this audience bring different routines, bodies, budgets, histories, preferences, and levels of access to every question.

After Forty exists to make room for a calmer kind of information. We break down labels, explain what a source actually studied, separate general context from product-level details, and name what remains uncertain. We take ordinary questions seriously without turning them into emergencies. The goal is not to prescribe a lifestyle. It is to help readers approach everyday choices with better questions and clearer expectations.

## Our editorial reason for being

The initiative is editorial rather than promotional: useful context should come before persuasion. In crowded categories, readers deserve to know the difference between an ingredient study and a finished product, between a listing claim and independent evidence, and between a possibility described in research and an outcome promised to an individual.

That distinction shapes the publication. We prefer measured language over hype, practical explanation over pressure, and attribution over authority-by-tone. We may discuss products, routines, or categories, but an article can remain useful without naming a product and no page in this draft asks a reader to buy anything. FBR News may support the publication through advertising and, where separately authorized, affiliate relationships; commercial arrangements do not determine the publication’s editorial conclusions.

## Who it is for

After Forty is for adults 40 and older, with a particular focus on readers 45+, in the United States and across a global English-speaking audience. It is for someone refining a routine, returning to movement, reading a label more carefully, or looking for grounded language around a personal-care and wellness decision. It is also for readers who are tired of being talked down to, rushed, or promised certainty where none exists.

You do not need to identify with a particular age, gender, fitness background, skin type, or spending level to read along. Bring your own context. Keep what is useful. Question what is not.

## What we cover

### Skin & Beauty

Ingredient literacy, routine basics, label reading, and the everyday decisions that sit between a product description and a personal preference. We aim for clear distinctions, not a single definition of what looking or feeling good should mean.

### Recovery & Wellness

Rest, movement, sleep, comfort, and the habits people use to support a sustainable routine. We describe general information with its limits and avoid turning broad wellness discussion into an individual instruction.

### Home Fitness

Accessible ways to think about movement at home, including the questions around protein and creatine for 40+. We keep ingredient or nutrient context separate from any particular product listing and do not use a category article to imply a universal routine, dose, or outcome.

## How we research and attribute evidence

We start with the specific statement we want to make, then ask what kind of source could reasonably support it. Depending on the topic, that may include public institutions, original research, systematic reviews, professional guidance, or clearly identified marketplace information. We record the source, access date, and relevant limitation. A manufacturer description can document what a label or listing says; it is not automatically independent support for a sensitive claim.

Internal source records are listed below for review. Public source links and access status must be verified before publication. We distinguish ingredient or general-context evidence (E1), product or listing facts (E2), and marketplace perception (E3). We do not treat an ingredient study as proof of a finished product, or an in-vitro or animal finding as evidence in humans. If the available evidence does not support the strength of a proposed statement, we narrow the wording, remove the statement, or pause the piece.

## What After Forty does not promise

This publication is for education and editorial context. It is **not medical advice**, diagnosis, or individualized care. Nothing here is a substitute for speaking with a qualified professional about your circumstances. We do not forecast an individual outcome or state that a routine, ingredient, product, or approach will work for you. Results vary, and uncertainty is part of an honest account.

## A note on the persona

Heidi Braun is a fictional editorial persona of FBR News. Her voice is a transparent design choice, not a claim of lived experience. The publication will keep that disclosure visible wherever the identity could otherwise be misunderstood.

## An editorial invitation

Start with the question you actually have. Read the explanation, follow the sources, and notice which details change the decision. If a topic needs stronger evidence or a more personal assessment, let that be the next question — not a reason to rush toward a promise.
