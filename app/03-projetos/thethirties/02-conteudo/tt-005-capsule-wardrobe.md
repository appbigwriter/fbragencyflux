## Article draft

### A Capsule Wardrobe for the Life You Actually Have

Somewhere in your thirties, your closet can become a very expensive archive of imaginary women: the woman who goes to rooftop dinners every Thursday, the woman who only wears linen, the woman who has never spilled coffee on herself before 9 a.m.

You do not need her wardrobe. You need clothes that cooperate with your actual life.

A capsule wardrobe is not a legally binding agreement to wear beige. It is a smaller, more intentional group of pieces that work across the situations you really move through: work, errands, dinners, travel, weather, and the occasional event where the invitation says “smart casual” and refuses to elaborate.

### Start with your week, not the internet

Before buying anything, list the places your clothes need to take you:

- work or client meetings;
- home and errands;
- movement or walks;
- dinners and social plans;
- travel;
- events or occasions.

Then look at what you already reach for. Those repeat choices are data. If you keep choosing the same comfortable trousers, shoes and layer, your closet is telling you something more useful than a trend report.

### The three-question filter

For every potential addition, ask:

1. **Can I wear it with at least three things I already own?**
2. **Does it work for a real situation on my calendar?**
3. **Will I reach for it when I am tired, late or mildly betrayed by the weather?**

If the answer is no to two of these, leave it in the shop. Your bank account is allowed to have boundaries too.

### Build around roles, not a magic number

There is no universal capsule number. A person who works from home, commutes, attends formal events or lives in a hot climate will need different pieces.

Think in roles:

- one or two reliable everyday bottoms;
- tops that work alone and under layers;
- one layer for temperature changes;
- shoes that match your walking reality;
- one outfit that makes an event easier;
- accessories that change the feeling of familiar basics.

The goal is not minimalism as performance. The goal is fewer “nothing to wear” moments created by clothes that do not cooperate with each other.

### Where accessories earn their place

Accessories can be the quiet overachievers of a small wardrobe. A belt, scarf, earrings, bag or shoe can change an outfit without asking you to buy an entirely new personality.

Choose by use first: comfort, durability, storage, weather and how often the item will leave the house. A beautiful bag that cannot hold your actual essentials is décor with a strap.

### What I would do

I would spend one week photographing the outfits I actually wear, then make a short “repeat list.” I would repair, tailor or clean the best pieces before shopping. After that, I would buy only the missing role—not the fantasy version of myself who apparently attends twelve weddings a year.

If dressing feels difficult because of body changes, stress or a demanding season, that is not a moral failure. Clothes are tools. You are already a whole person before the outfit arrives.

## Video script

### Title
**I Built a Capsule Wardrobe for My Actual Life (Not My Imaginary Calendar)**

### Hook — 0:00–0:20

“Welcome to the closet of a woman in her thirties: six black tops, three versions of the same hopeful outfit, and one dress waiting for an event that has not happened since 2019. Today we are building a capsule wardrobe for real life—not a beige personality transplant.”

### Segment 1 — Audit — 0:20–1:30

Show the closet and explain the weekly-use audit. Pull out the pieces worn repeatedly. Say: “These are not boring clothes. These are evidence.”

### Segment 2 — The roles — 1:30–3:00

Create five sections: everyday, work, movement, social, weather/travel. Explain that the number of pieces depends on lifestyle and climate; there is no universal formula.

### Segment 3 — Three-question filter — 3:00–4:15

Demonstrate three possible purchases and ask:

- Can it make three outfits with what I already own?
- Does it serve a real calendar situation?
- Would I choose it on a tired Tuesday?

### Segment 4 — Accessories — 4:15–5:00

Show how one accessory changes two existing outfits. Avoid “must-have” language. Mention comfort, durability and actual capacity.

### Segment 5 — What I’d do — 5:00–5:40

“I would wait a week before buying anything, photograph the outfits I actually wear, and purchase only the missing role. The goal is fewer decisions, not a closet that wins an argument online.”
