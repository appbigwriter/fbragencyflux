## Executive decision

SharpEye should own the intersection of **physical brand presence + business consequence** rather than compete head-on for generic “trade show booth” catalog terms. The editorial wedge is a diagnostic voice that explains why a space is ignored, confusing, or forgettable and identifies the highest-leverage change.

Primary editorial promise:

> Diagnose the space. Clarify the message. Make the right people stop.

The first content system should combine:

1. high-intent service/problem queries around trade-show displays and signage;
2. educational queries around visual merchandising and retail layout;
3. diagnostic “why is my booth/store not working?” queries;
4. Orlando/Florida modifiers only where the commercial or fulfillment context is truthful;
5. The Teardown as a repeatable editorial format, using authorized, composite, or public examples.

## Evidence and limitations

Live retrieved sources establish the vocabulary and user problems below:

- Shopify defines visual merchandising through displays, layout, lighting, décor, branding, and experiential cues that guide exploration and buying; it also emphasizes focal points and natural foot-traffic flow.
- EXHIBITOR states that small-booth graphics must communicate who the exhibitor is, what it sells, and the benefit in a three-to-five-second glance; it recommends concise copy, benefit-led wording, contrast, readable type, and strong imagery.
- SpeedPro Orlando South positions local trade-show display work around booth layouts, graphics, rapid production, installation, delivery, and Central Florida convention needs.
- ExpoMarketing positions Orlando exhibit work around custom, modular, portable, and rental displays, with budget/flexibility arguments.
- Lunne positions experiential design around trade-show booths, corporate-office branding, strategy, spatial design, production, and commercial impact.

## Editorial architecture from intent

- Pillar 1 — Stop: why people walk past a booth/store and how to improve first-glance clarity.
- Pillar 2 — Read: signage, hierarchy, contrast, sightlines, and message compression.
- Pillar 3 — Move: layout, threshold, traffic flow, focal points, and interaction.
- Pillar 4 — Trust: professionalism, consistency, proof, accessibility, and operational finish.
- Pillar 5 — Choose: portable/modular/custom/rental decisions without pretending one format fits every business.
- Series — The Teardown: one authorized/public/composite space, observed behavior, one priority fix, clear limits.

## Claims and compliance guardrails

- Do not claim that a display will increase sales, leads, foot traffic, or ROI without a cited study or the client’s measured data.
- Do not use live small-business spaces without permission; prefer composites, public examples, or anonymized authorized submissions.
- Disclose product/affiliate relationships near links; relevance comes before commission.
