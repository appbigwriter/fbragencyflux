# 10 Things to Attract Customers to Your Business

**Audience:** Small businesses in the United States and Canada  
**Primary promise:** Simple, affordable strategies to turn passersby into customers—and customers into contacts who come back.

Every business has a physical customer touchpoint: a storefront, counter, reception desk, table or booth. That space can work as media when it helps people notice, understand and act.

## 1. Say clearly what you do
Pair the business name with a plain-language category. A passerby should understand the offer before interpreting the logo.

## 2. Give people one useful reason to stop
Use a truthful current offer, seasonal item, consultation, event or useful resource. One clear reason beats five competing messages.

## 3. Make opening hours visible
Unclear hours create uncertainty. Place them where people decide whether to approach the entrance.

## 4. Improve the first three seconds
Use a hierarchy: what you offer, who it helps or what is available now, and the next action.

## 5. Match the message to the location
Exterior copy helps someone decide whether to enter. Counter copy supports an add-on. Reception copy encourages booking, following or returning.

## 6. Design for distance and readability
Review type size, contrast, spacing, glare and viewing distance. Check local accessibility and sign rules before production.

## 7. Use real proof
Reviews, customer-created content and verifiable credentials can reduce uncertainty. Never invent testimonials or unsupported claims.

## 8. Offer a low-friction next step
Invite a menu scan, guide download, loyalty sign-up, quote request or follow. Explain the benefit before the QR code.

## 9. Place add-ons where decisions happen
A specific prompt beside the register or on a table can help customers notice a relevant complement without pressure.

## 10. Measure one change at a time
Record a baseline: walk-ins, featured-item sales, add-on rate, QR scans, sign-ups or reviews. Change one variable and compare a reasonable window.

## Ten-minute audit

- Can I identify the business quickly?
- Can I tell whether it is open?
- Is there one clear reason to stop?
- Is the main message readable at the relevant distance?
- Is the next action obvious?
- Is a QR-code benefit clear?
- Is the add-on prompt at the decision point?
- Can the message be updated?
- Is every claim supportable?
- What result will I measure next week?

Store Signs & Displays are implementation tools, not magic products. Choose an A-frame, counter display, QR sign or printed piece only after defining the customer decision it should support.
